The Problem

Wind energy's dirty secret.

Wind turbines are built to be clean. The blades are not built to be recyclable. Made of glass fiber composite — glass fiber, epoxy resin, thermosets, balsa wood, and PVC — they resist every conventional recycling method. Until now, the only answer was the landfill.

NOW — 2025
3,000–9,000 blades retiring annually across the United States. First wave of large-scale wind farm decommissions underway. Most blades are cut into thirds and buried whole.
2028
Composite blade waste projected to reach 330,000 tons/year globally. Landfill bans already adopted in parts of Europe. US regulatory pressure building fast.
2028–2040
Annual global blade waste rises to 418,000 tons/year. US retirements accelerate to 10,000–20,000 blades per year. Without a solution, this is a liability on every wind energy balance sheet.
2050 PROJECTION
$0 tons to landfill — if Seebury's total solution scales to meet the wave. The infrastructure and technology exist today. The only missing ingredient is deployment at scale.

A

Longhorn Solar · Structural Reuse

Blade composite becomes solar infrastructure

1
Blade Assessment — Decommissioned blades evaluated for structural integrity and composite composition at the wind farm site
2
Fabrication — Viable composite sections cut and prepared as vertical fence panel substrates using Longhorn Solar's proprietary process
3
Solar Integration — Vertical solar panels, IP cameras, ground sensors, and fiber mounted to the fence spine — blade material becomes the structural backbone
4
Deployment — Installed as a full C4ISR perimeter system generating up to 5 MW per 10 acres. The blade's second life begins — and it generates revenue
5
End of Second Life — When the fence eventually retires, remaining material enters Path B with Carbon Rivers for molecular reclamation. True closed loop.
B

Carbon Rivers · Glass to Glass Reclamation

Blade composite returns to manufacturing supply chain

1
Receive & Pre-Sort — Bulk composite waste received from wind farm decommissions. Blades pre-sorted and shredded to optimal feedstock size
2
Multi-Stage Processing — Carbon Rivers' proprietary process fully removes polymer, char, and fillers from glass fibers — restoring fiber properties to virgin-like values at 99.9% purity
3
Glass Fiber Recovery — Clean, mechanically intact glass fibers recovered as non-woven mats, surface veils, thermoplastic pellets, and composite intermediates
4
Return to Manufacturing — Reclaimed fiber sold into turbine blade manufacturing, automotive, marine, aerospace, and construction supply chains — displacing virgin glass at competitive prices
5
Materials Loop Closed — Glass fiber that started in a turbine blade ends up in the next turbine blade. The first fully circular economy for glass fiber composites.
